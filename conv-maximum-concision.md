Corpus prose states an idea in the fewest words that keep its meaning whole.
Where two phrasings carry the same meaning, the shorter is the agreed form; where the shorter drops a distinction, it has not kept the meaning and the longer stands.

- [Say it plainly](./prin-say-it-plainly.md){id=authoring:prin:h8rz1tm} settles how a sentence reads.
- [Earned substance](./prin-earned-substance.md){id=authoring:prin:bmgx60e} settles whether a passage is written at all.

This convention settles how long the passage surviving both runs. It governs length alone. 

So it bites on:
- words that carry no substance;
- restated clause, the second example that teaches nothing the first did not;
- the qualifier that narrows nothing;
- the sentence announcing what the next sentence says.

## Rationale

Every word is read far more often than it is written, cold and at machine speed, by newcomers and by agents holding one subset of the files.
Length is a cost the reader pays and the author does not, so the work of cutting sits with the author.
