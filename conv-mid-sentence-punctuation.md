Corpus prose breaks a long sentence at its natural joints instead of stitching independent clauses together.
A full stop starts a new sentence; a comma sets off a light pause or a subordinate clause; parentheses hold a dispensable aside; a semicolon joins two independent clauses close enough in meaning to share one sentence; a colon introduces only what completes, explains, or lists the clause before it.

A dash, whether an em-dash or a spaced hyphen used as one, plays none of these roles and is not used as mid-sentence punctuation.
Swapping it for a colon or semicolon that welds two clauses which do not complete, explain, or closely relate is the same fault in different punctuation; where they do not relate, a full stop is correct instead.

The rule governs mid-sentence punctuation only.
A hyphen joining a compound word (pain-reliever, gain-creator) is correct and untouched, and a lone dash in a table cell marking "none" or "not applicable" is allowed.
