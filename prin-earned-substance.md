An artefact, a section, or a sentence earns its place only if it carries meaning nothing else carries.
When in doubt, do not add.

## Why this matters

Unearned content buries what matters, taxes every reader and maintainer, and erodes trust. Once readers notice padding, they discount everything.
Fluent writers over-produce by default, capable AI agents above all, so the stronger the writer, the harder this principle must be applied.

## How to apply

Before adding anything, ask two questions: does it say something nothing else says (including other artefacts in the corpus), and would removing it lose information?
If either answer is no, do not add it.

Watch for the common offenders: a gain that only negates a pain already recorded, a section written to complete a template, prose that talks a lot and says little.
In review, delete what fails the test; do not soften it.

## Considered alternative

Full explicitness, spelling everything out wherever the reader might want it: discarded.
The cost of following a reference is a click; the cost of padding is noise, maintenance, and lost trust.

## Origin

Cousins in YAGNI, KISS, and load-bearing structure in architecture. Only what bears load earns its place.
